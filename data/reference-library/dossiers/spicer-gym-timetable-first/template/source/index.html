The induction is free and it is not a sales pitch.

The people who would benefit most from a gym are the ones most put off by walking into one. That is worth saying plainly rather than papering over with another photograph of somebody mid-deadlift.

You can book it before joining, and if you never come back nobody will ring you.

01

It is free and it is an hour

Booked whenever suits, including before you join. No obligation attached to it.

02

A coach walks the floor with you

Where things live, how the racks adjust, which machines are worth your time.

03

You lift something, badly

That is the point. Getting corrected once at the start saves months of guessing.

04

You leave with a starting plan

Three sessions written down. Not a sales pitch — an actual plan on paper.

Questions

Asked before joining, every time.

Is there a contract?+

No. Monthly rolling, no joining fee, one month's notice by email, and no retention call. The terms are printed on this page rather than in a PDF you find after signing.

Will I be the least fit person there?+

Almost certainly not, and the induction exists precisely for this. It is a free hour with a coach who shows you where everything is and how to use it, and nobody is watching you.

Can I try before joining?+

A day pass is available and its cost comes off your first month if you join within a fortnight.

How busy does it get?+

17:30 to 19:30 on weekdays is the peak and the platforms go first. Before 08:00 and after 20:30 you will usually have a rack to yourself.

Do you have a beginner programme?+

The induction ends with three written sessions. Beyond that the Full membership includes the programme template library, which is where most people start.
