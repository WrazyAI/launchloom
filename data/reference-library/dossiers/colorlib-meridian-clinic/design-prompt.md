# Reference implementation brief: Meridian clinical utility

## Scope and non-copy rule

Use the screenshots as evidence for information hierarchy and page composition, not as a source of business facts, clinical copy, branding, or patient photography. Do not reuse Meridian's identity, sample city, address, phone, emergency numbers, doctors, qualifications, services, insurers, hours, wait times, outcome figures, or medical statements. Treat the reference image as a CC0 skyline placeholder only, not as a depiction of the client clinic. Never use the gradient clinician placeholders as if they show actual staff. All client content must come from sealed, verified tokens, and healthcare claims require appropriate client approval.

## Visual hierarchy

Start with a compact utility strip for genuinely useful contact or status information, followed by a restrained wordmark navigation. In the opening, balance a concise, patient-centered promise and two clear actions against a narrower appointment-request panel. The booking area is visually integrated with the hero, not buried at the page end. Give the form a clean surface, clear field labels, visible consent language where needed, keyboard focus states, and an honest note about what happens after submission.

Use a bright clinical palette: white and pale cool surfaces, deep blue-green ink, and a restrained teal accent. Typography should be a highly legible sans with strong size hierarchy and comfortable line-height. After the hero, present services as a scannable icon-led directory with short factual descriptions. If the client has verified staff biographies, credentials, or payment information, place them in a quieter trust chapter; otherwise omit those sections rather than inventing filler. Close with patient questions and contact/hours in a two-part layout, then a compact footer.

## Page sequence

Use this information sequence: verified utility strip; compact navigation; hero promise with an embedded inquiry panel; service directory; optional verified team or practice details; optional verified payment/coverage notes; patient FAQ beside contact and hours; footer. Preserve the movement from immediate action to service understanding to practical reassurance. Do not force provider profiles, insurance logos, patient portals, diagnostics, stats, or pricing into businesses that do not have those verified facts. For a dental practice or other regulated healthcare provider, adapt terminology and navigation to the actual practice type and approved content.

## Interaction and motion

Prefer browser-native and transparent interactions: normal links, associated labels, clear inline validation, native disclosure for FAQs, and a service selector only when connected to a real form handler. A static request form must state that it sends a request and does not confirm an appointment. Do not collect sensitive health details in a marketing inquiry. Emergency guidance, contact numbers, and service availability are jurisdiction-specific; show them only when supplied and reviewed. Avoid parallax, animated urgency, autoplay, or motion that competes with healthcare information. Reduced-motion users must see the same content and working controls.

## Responsive translation

At 1536 by 864, the opening should expose the main promise, booking action, and beginning of the appointment path without making the form dominate the page. At 390 by 844, stack hero copy and form in a clear reading order; keep labels, inputs, consent, and submit action accessible without a tiny scroll trap. Collapse the service directory based on actual text lengths. FAQ disclosures and opening hours must remain easy to tap and read. Never let a sticky utility strip obscure content, and confirm no horizontal overflow at 390 pixels.

## Signature elements

Preserve this family's visual grammar: a factual utility strip, direct navigation, a split hero with an adjacent booking panel, a scannable icon-led service directory, and a practical patient-information/contact close. The photo treatment is deliberately secondary. Prefer client clinic images; if there are none, use neutral, rights-cleared healthcare imagery only as generic atmosphere and do not imply that it shows this practice. The visual system should remain calm and useful rather than becoming a generic SaaS dashboard.

## Prohibited patterns

Never copy the source clinic's name or content, fabricate clinicians or qualifications, invent appointment availability or response times, imply emergency coverage without verified facts, claim outcomes, or suggest a form has booked an appointment when it has not. Do not use the skyline as the clinic's address context, gradients as staff portraits, or stock patients as testimonials. Do not collect unnecessary sensitive health data. Do not use color as the only status cue, hide FAQ content behind hover, or inflate the page with fabricated statistics, insurance logos, generic trust badges, or unsupported claims.

## Local SEO and conversion

Bind practice name, specialty, services, city, address, phone, hours, accessibility, insurance/payment, and clinician data only to verified fields. Put one primary appointment-request action near the opening promise and repeat it after service information where useful. Make visible contact information agree with LocalBusiness/MedicalBusiness data and the canonical URL. Use descriptive service headings and answer practical patient questions in plain language, without diagnostic advice. Never add em dashes.
