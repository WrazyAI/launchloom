# Local auto-repair diagnostic-first implementation brief

Use the paired screenshots as reference for a neighborhood repair shop's diagnostic journey, not for vehicle-manufacturer product marketing. Build a new experience for an independent garage using the client's confirmed services, supported makes, labor/diagnostic fees, hours, inspection process, certifications, and service area. Do not copy the Spicer name, shop name, source writing, source hero image, service prices, warranty/legal claims, reviews, or vehicle coverage. Use client-owned photographs first; otherwise source or generate a new licensed shop image, clearly treated as illustration rather than a claim about the client's real premises.

## Visual hierarchy

Open with a dark service-bay image, a concise diagnosis-first promise, a clear diagnostic/booking action, a secondary symptom prompt, and a small facts strip containing only verified information. Use a narrow utility bar for phone and hours. This should look like a local workshop that explains how it examines a vehicle, not an automaker campaign, dealership catalog, or car-launch landing page. Keep one signal-red or amber action color and technical labels restrained. The main hierarchy should move from symptom, to possible service path, to documented inspection and approval.

The source intends Inter as its body/display font but the static HTML's font utility fails to set `font-family`; its screenshot therefore resolves to a system sans. If using Inter or another selected font, set the font family explicitly and verify it loads. Use dark graphite surfaces, warm white text, and crisp data panels. A real shop photo should remain atmospheric and should not compete with the diagnostic promise.

## Page sequence

1. Compact utility bar with verified hours and phone/call route.
2. Repair-bay hero with a diagnosis-first promise, diagnostic request action, and an optional symptom prompt.
3. Small symptom-to-service triage using common problems the business actually handles. Always offer an unsure/contact path and avoid giving a remote diagnosis.
4. Clear service capability list with a scope boundary for each real service.
5. Transparent labor/diagnostic fee basis and supported vehicle makes, only where the client verifies them.
6. Digital inspection explanation: what evidence the customer receives, how estimates and approvals work, and how urgent versus deferrable work is distinguished, using the client's real process.
7. Repair commitments and warranty policy, only if documented and current.
8. Genuine, approved reviews, concise FAQ, and a diagnostic request form connected to a real destination.
9. Contact/location footer using a real address or service area distinction; never imply a physical office if the business is mobile or area-based.

The order reflects how people decide whether to trust a mechanic: their symptom, your relevant expertise, fee clarity, and what they will see before authorizing a repair. Use local SEO terms grounded in the verified repair categories, supported vehicle types, and service area. Do not claim expertise in a make, repair, warranty, certification, or safety result without evidence. Any legal warranty or consumer-rights statement must be approved for the client's jurisdiction before publication.

## Responsive translation

At 1440px, the repair-bay hero can pair its headline/action block with a compact fact strip. Follow with a two-column symptom-to-service module, then a service grid and a clear inspection story. At 390px, put the utility bar first, stack the hero text before the primary image if that improves first-screen fit, and place symptom choices above their explanation. Reflow labor prices into labeled rows, list supported vehicle makes without sideways scrolling, and present inspection steps vertically. Keep phone, diagnostic request, and emergency/scheduled distinctions in the reading order.

Use transitions only to clarify selection state or progression through inspection steps. When reduced motion is enabled, expose all symptoms and inspection stages statically. The source HTML has scroll-reveal classes without an observer script, so do not repeat hidden-by-default content. A symptom matcher must not assert a diagnosis or repair price automatically. Make any estimate conditional on real inspection and show where client authorization happens. Keep the form accessible, keyboard-operable, and honest about submission.

## Signature elements

- A local workshop image and diagnostic-first opening, not manufacturer marketing.
- A short, evidence-led symptom-to-service route with a clear unsure option.
- A transparent labor/diagnostic fee explanation near service detail.
- A digital-inspection panel showing the real report, approval, or prioritization process.
- A concise repair-commitment or supported-makes ledger grounded in client evidence.

## Prohibited patterns

- No automaker logos, vehicle campaign photography, fictional shop identity, source writing, source image, sample prices, reviews, makes-serviced list, warranties, or operating hours.
- No diagnosis from a self-reported symptom, safety guarantee, fabricated repair result, diagnostic accuracy claim, labor promise, warranty/legal assertion, or certification.
- No invented live status, appointment availability, service area, physical address, towing, loaner vehicle, or same-day completion.
- No car-manufacturer marketing grammar, feature-comparison catalogue, generic round-card grid, or fake digital inspection screenshot.
- No button or form that appears to work without a real route, no inaccessible symptom color coding, and no content hidden by a missing reveal script.

Before delivery, check every service, fee, make, credential, warranty, hour, location, and review against sealed verified business facts. Test the symptom choices, call link, and form; inspect mobile for zero overflow; verify reduced motion and semantic accessibility; and confirm that the page presents a local repair provider rather than a vehicle brand.
