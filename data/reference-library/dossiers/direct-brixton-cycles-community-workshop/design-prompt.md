# Reference implementation brief: community-led bicycle workshop

Use the paired direct-site screenshots as evidence for a neighborhood bicycle workshop and shop, not as a page to reproduce. Transfer the information architecture, editorial pacing, and service transparency described below. Do not reproduce the source business name, logo, exact typeface, copy, price list, photography, reviews, reported history, rating, claims, or trade dress. This is a direct-source reference whose screenshots and derived prompt are retained under requester-attested internal visual-reference clearance; no source-owner grant or individual image/font license was supplied. Use client-owned assets first and separately cleared media otherwise.

## Visual hierarchy

Build an expressive local mobility identity from the client's verified character. The reference uses a near-black page, large condensed display typography, sharp white labels, and a bright yellow action color. A documentary workshop image establishes the setting before service details. Keep the action visible against photography with a clear contrast treatment. Pair large chapter titles with compact utility labels, then use practical service descriptions and prices at a calm reading size. The source opens with a bold editorial statement, a small set of identity facts, and a repeated text strip before separating three broad needs: repair, retail, and community. Treat these as distinct visitor paths. Do not reproduce the source's phrases, exact colors, wordmark, or typeface. If a selected font is not client-owned or system-provided, use a separately licensed font and record its exact source and license.

Every fact block must come from the sealed client record. Do not invent an opening year, ownership model, rating, years of operation, community membership, locations, staff identities, prices, or workshop capabilities. The source title and story disagree about co-operative history and newer stewardship, so do not present its history as verified. A photographic technician or shop should be shown only when the client supplied and approved that image; never present stock or generated people as the business's employees or customers.

## Page sequence

1. Compact navigation with the business name, service/shop/community routes, and one useful contact action.
2. Documentary workshop opening with a concise local service promise, one primary booking or enquiry action, and a secondary service-pricing route.
3. Optional proof strip using only verified business facts. A static typographic band can separate the opening from the content; omit or neutralize the moving source marquee unless it adds a clear, accessible purpose.
4. A small service directory that distinguishes repair, products, and community activity only when each exists for the client.
5. Retail or available-bike information only when linked to current, confirmed stock. Keep repair photography separate from product records.
6. A transparent service and price ledger. State what each service includes, whether parts cost extra, and how bespoke work is quoted. Use real client prices or omit figures.
7. A brief workshop or business story paired with client-approved process photography and facts.
8. Local partners or community programs only if verified and approved.
9. Genuine customer proof only with permission and a current source. Close with address or service-area details, accurate hours, phone or message route, and a callback form connected to a real destination.

Avoid repeating the same service claim across the hero, pillars, price ledger, and footer. Give each chapter a distinct job. Where the client has one workshop, state the verified physical address. A mobile service area is not a second physical location.

## Responsive translation

At 390px, preserve the compact navigation and opening promise, then stack the action before the next major image or service entry. Keep the service directory in a short vertical sequence. Present prices, inclusions, and parts exclusions as readable rows or stacked groups; do not reduce the desktop ledger to tiny columns. Make the contact action and fields thumb-friendly, associate every form label with its input, and keep buttons keyboard-operable. Do not require hover to reveal service information. Keep any photographic mosaic in a deliberate single-column sequence with consistent spacing.

The source's live document measures 393px wide at a 390px viewport because its repeated text marquee extends beyond the right edge. Preserve the observed behavior in the evidence record, but do not copy that overflow. Clip any decorative strip to its container, keep document width at the viewport, and verify at both 390px and 320px. Prefer a static strip under reduced motion; if motion is used, honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and do not make content depend on it. Recheck the full page for horizontal scrolling, readable type, loaded client imagery, and actions hidden behind sticky UI.

## Signature elements

- A high-impact typographic opening grounded in an authentic local workshop image, with one prominent next step.
- A small directory that distinguishes service, retail, and community paths instead of repeating generic feature cards.
- A service price ledger that names inclusions and parts or quote conditions in plain language.
- Varied documentary images used to explain the workshop and its work, sourced only from the client or cleared independently.
- A local closing section combining the accurate visit or service-area facts with a direct, honest contact route.

Use these mechanics as a family of design decisions, not as a fixed template. The client recipe, business kind, real assets, and verified facts decide the final composition.

## Prohibited patterns

- No source name, logo, tagline, headline, copy, layout trace, staff, photography, reviews, exact price list, claimed service history, or rating.
- No unverified co-op or collective claim, ownership history, opening year, business longevity, review count, star rating, service price, popular-service label, shop address, opening hours, response time, warranty, or service promise.
- No source photographs or fonts as client assets, and no stock person framed as a real mechanic, rider, customer, or employee.
- No fake inventory records, fake prices, fake review cards, decorative counters, or empty community-partner logos.
- No generic rounded-card wall, unreadable microcopy, hover-only service details, inaccessible contrast, or horizontal page overflow.
- No moving strip that traps focus, obscures meaning, or ignores reduced-motion settings. No form that appears to submit without a verified destination.

## Local SEO and conversion

Use the verified service category customers search for locally, such as bicycle repair or bicycle servicing, only if that is the client's actual offer. Do not conflate bicycle, e-bike, motorcycle, and car service. Keep name, address, and phone consistent with the client record and structured local-business data. Distinguish a physical workshop from pickup coverage or a mobile service area. Use the primary action the client can fulfill, such as calling, requesting a repair slot, or asking for a quote. Show pricing only when current and approved, explain any parts or inspection conditions beside the price, and keep the form short enough for mobile completion.
