# Reference implementation brief: Emergency plumber dispatch

## Scope and non-copy rule

Use the paired licensed captures to study the emergency-to-planned-work split, the dispatch-console geometry, and the service-rate presentation. The source template is CC BY 4.0, with its license and attribution kept in this dossier. Do not reuse the fictional Meridian brand, phone, prices, ETA, credentials, reviews, or claims. Its hero artwork is AI-generated placeholder art. All client identity, locations, prices, service times, and search copy must come from sealed verified intake and SEO tokens.

## Visual hierarchy

Start with a very narrow status strip, a compact trade wordmark and direct phone route, then a deep-navy split hero. Emergency copy and one primary action occupy the left; a right-side instrument panel shows only client-supplied operational data or a clearly labeled illustration. Use cyan as a functional signal rather than a generic gradient, and reserve yellow for a high-priority action. Keep the headline compact and the opening within one desktop viewport.

## Page sequence

Immediately after the hero, present three routes: active emergency, planned service, and unsure/diagnostic. Follow with a category-filtered flat-rate table, a brief explanation of what the diagnostic covers, a factual coverage/service-area visual, concise questions answered directly in the page, and a form with only fields needed to begin dispatch. Keep emergency and non-emergency paths distinct.

## Interaction and motion

The source console, service filters, and zone diagram are visual mechanics. Use filters that work with keyboard and touch, a static console fallback, and a service-radius drawing only when location data supports it. Do not simulate a live dispatcher, changing truck count, or arrival ETA.

## Responsive translation

At 390px, place the emergency promise and phone/request action first, then the console. Stack triage options into clear touch rows. Convert the price table into readable service rows with scope, duration, and price as separate token-driven fields. Replace a desktop zone map with a factual text list. There must be no horizontal scroll.

## Signature elements

Preserve the dispatch-console hero, the three-path emergency triage, and a flat-rate service index. Use separate surfaces for the urgent route and planned work rather than repeating identical cards.

## Prohibited patterns

Never copy fictional prices, dispatch times, employee certifications, reviews, response guarantees, or geographic coverage. Avoid placing all service details inside accordions, using an invented live-status gauge, or implying the business is open or dispatched in real time.

## Local SEO and conversion

Use the client’s actual plumbing services and verified service area in headings, rows, and FAQs. Put the emergency phone route in the opening, then make booking and scheduled maintenance available at their decision points. Keep title/meta/structured-data content derived from researched client facts, not from the template’s demo details.
