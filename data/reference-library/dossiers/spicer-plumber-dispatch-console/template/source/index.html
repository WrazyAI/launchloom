Before you call

Is this an emergency?

Half the calls we take at 2am could safely wait until 8am, and the other half needed the water off five minutes ago. Here is how to tell which one you have.

1

Shut it off

Water still moving means damage still spreading. Do this first, before you look for a phone.

  1. 01Find the main: garage wall, basement front wall, or a meter box at the kerb on the street side.
  2. 02Round handwheel turns clockwise until it stops — usually six or seven full turns, not one.
  3. 03Lever handle turns a quarter turn only, until it sits across the pipe rather than along it.
  4. 04Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the standing pressure out of the risers.
  5. 05If the valve is seized, do not force it. Shut the fixture stop under the sink or toilet instead and tell us on the call.
2

Call now

Anything on this list gets a van tonight. Do not wait for morning and do not wait for the water to stop on its own.

  • Water actively running and the main will not close
  • Sewage backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain
  • Any smell of gas near the water heater — leave first, then call
  • Ceiling sagging, staining or dripping from the floor above
  • Water near a panel, outlet or the heater's electrical supply
  • No water at all in freezing weather — a line is frozen and may already be split

3

Can wait

Book these for a normal weekday slot and you skip the after-hours dispatch fee entirely. We will say so on the call rather than take the overtime.

  • One slow drain, with the others running fine
  • A toilet that runs on but still flushes
  • Dripping faucet or a weeping shutoff you can bucket
  • Water heater that recovers slowly but still heats
  • Low pressure at a single fixture
  • Planned work — repipe, remodel rough-in, fixture swap

There is an after-hours dispatch fee and you hear the number on the call, before we send anybody. It is never added to the invoice afterwards, and it is waived entirely if the repair turns out to be covered under our own workmanship warranty.

Two vans stay staffed overnight. Inside the 45-minute band that is a genuine 45 minutes most nights; if the on-call van is already on a job, dispatch tells you the honest number instead of the number you want to hear.

Always, and the permit fee is already inside the flat price. An unpermitted heater is the single most common thing that blows up a home sale inspection three years later, so it is not a corner worth cutting to save you ninety dollars.
06What if you open a wall and find worse damage?
Work stops and you get a photo, a revised flat price and the option to say no. You are only committed to the original scope. If you decline the extra work we make the area safe, cap the line and charge the original number.
Right now, before we arrive

Six things that limit the damage.

None of this needs a tool and all of it saves money. Work down the list while the van is en route — most of the difference between a $400 repair and a $4,000 restoration job happens in the twenty minutes before anybody gets there.

  1. 01Shut the water offMain valve first, then the fixture stop if the main is seized. Full instructions and a diagram are in the triage panel above.
  2. 02Kill power to wet roomsIf water is anywhere near outlets, a panel or a water heater, throw the breaker for that circuit before you wade in.
  3. 03Open a low faucetA basement or ground-floor tap drains the standing pressure out of the risers so the leak slows while you wait.
  4. 04Photograph everythingWide shot, then close. Insurance wants the before, and a photo of the fitting lets us load the right part on the van.
  5. 05Move what you can liftRugs, boxes, anything on a wooden floor. Standing water wicks upward into subfloor faster than most people expect.
  6. 06Leave the access clearPath to the meter, the heater and the crawl hatch. Every minute we spend shifting storage is a minute we are not fixing the leak.
