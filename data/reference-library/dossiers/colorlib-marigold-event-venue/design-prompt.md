# Reference implementation brief: Marigold invitation-led events

## Scope and non-copy rule

Treat the desktop and mobile screenshots as evidence of composition, sequence, and interaction mechanics only. Do not recreate the Marigold wordmark, wedding couple, prose, date, venue, schedule, travel notes, guest names, gifts, or recognizable brand treatment. Adapt the visual grammar for a real event venue, caterer, or planner using only sealed client facts and approved assets. A demo RSVP is not a booking system. Do not claim that submitting an inquiry reserves a date unless the real backend confirms it.

## Visual hierarchy

Begin with an immersive, full-viewport image treated like an invitation poster. Put the event or venue proposition in a centered, high-contrast typographic stack, with concise metadata and one direct next step. The hero should feel composed and human, not like a dashboard or conventional split column. Follow it with a more intimate story chapter that pairs one portrait or place image with a restrained editorial text column. Use warm paper backgrounds, generous whitespace, quiet dividers, and a high-contrast serif display paired with a neutral sans for practical information.

The event program is a main information chapter, not a set of equal cards. Give times or planning phases a narrow visual rail, and keep each description short and scannable. After the schedule, use an irregular photo sequence to restore atmosphere. End the conversion path with a distinct inquiry or availability form, then practical guest or visit details and a compact footer. The precise sections must be selected for the business: remove guest-only wedding content from a venue site, and add only genuine venue, catering, accessibility, or travel details.

## Page sequence

Use this transferable sequence: invitation-style hero; concise story or venue point of view; ruled timeline for a verified event schedule or planning process; asymmetric photo mosaic; availability or event inquiry; practical event/visit information; footer. For an event venue, the “timeline” may describe how an inquiry becomes a tour or booking only when the actual process is confirmed. A planner can use it for verified planning milestones. A catering business may replace it with service formats or event-day logistics. Do not force a wedding story, gift section, accommodation advice, or ceremony timetable onto unrelated businesses.

## Interaction and motion

Keep the main journey available as normal page content. Use a native form with clear labels and helpful validation. A countdown is permitted only when an exact future date is supplied by the client and its value is useful to visitors. Otherwise show static availability language sourced from verified facts. If an optional countdown is used, it must not announce false scarcity, and it must respect reduced-motion settings. Navigation can use a keyboard-operable drawer on small screens. Do not use autoplay video, scroll-jacking, hover-only logistics, or animation that hides schedule and contact information.

## Responsive translation

At 1536 by 864, keep the entire event identity, essential date/availability cue, and primary action inside the opening screen. Ensure the text stays legible against the image with a stable contrast layer. At 390 by 844, retain the invitation-poster feeling without clipping the title or placing actions over key image content. Stack story image and copy, keep time labels separate from timeline text, and reshape the mosaic into a deliberate vertical rhythm with more than one image scale. Stack inquiry fields and contact details. There must be no horizontal overflow, clipped controls, or unexpectedly tall fixed elements.

## Signature elements

Preserve four linked mechanics: centered photographic invitation, short editorial story, clear ruled event timeline, and irregular photo chapter leading to a distinct inquiry step. The first and last sections should feel like bookends, but not duplicate one another. Use client photography when available. Every demo image must remain clearly a reference asset or be replaced with an asset whose rights and subject fit the actual client.

## Prohibited patterns

Do not reuse names, dates, locations, schedules, prices, travel advice, gift requests, client stories, reviews, event capacity, or booking promises from the source. Do not fabricate availability or use an arbitrary countdown. Do not build fake booking, RSVP, or payment behavior. Do not present CC0 photographs as the client's place or past customers. Avoid generic equal-sized card grids, false urgency, dense navigation, unsupported claims, or a long block of undifferentiated “why us” copy.

## Local SEO and conversion

Use the actual business name, city/service area, address, phone, email, event types, accessibility details, pricing policy, and booking process only when verified. Put the primary inquiry action near the top and again after visitors have seen the relevant event information. Use descriptive headings and meaningful internal links for services, FAQs, and contact. Schema markup, canonical URL, and visible business facts must agree with the sealed site configuration. Never add em dashes.
