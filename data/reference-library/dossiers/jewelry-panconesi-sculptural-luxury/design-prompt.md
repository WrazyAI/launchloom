# Sculptural luxury editorial-commerce implementation brief

Use the paired direct-site captures as evidence of image composition, whitespace, retail taxonomy, and pacing. Build an original experience for the client's verified luxury or independent-retail business. Do not reproduce Panconesi's name, logo, exact language, products, product names, site assets, source photography, fonts, or trade dress. Use client-owned imagery first. If none exists, generate or source new imagery under approved rights, with the correct product category and material facts.

## Visual hierarchy

Open with an art-directed portrait diptych or a similarly strong pairing of body-worn product photographs. Let the images introduce the product world before you show a long product grid. Use a thin announcement line and a compact wordmark header. Keep search, shop, and cart actions available without turning navigation into a heavy pill-shaped bar. Place a brief editorial statement on a clean band after the portraits; allow that band to interrupt the photographic rhythm and give the visitor a moment to understand the brand's verified point of view.

Use a restrained palette of warm white, paper gray, charcoal text, and material color drawn from actual approved photography. Typography should be refined but legible: expressive serif for a centered statement or product story; neutral sans serif for navigation, taxonomic labels, policies, and checkout details. Product images should feel tactile and sculptural. Use precise crops, generous whitespace, and a limited number of images with intentional changes of scale. Do not add low-contrast gray text simply to mimic a luxury mood.

## Page sequence

1. A discreet announcement ticker only if it communicates a verified shipping, appointment, event, or service fact; otherwise omit it.
2. Minimal brand navigation with shop/category, search, and cart actions.
3. Portrait diptych or paired product-use images as the opening image composition.
4. A separate centered editorial statement of one or two short sentences grounded in the client's actual positioning.
5. An edge-to-edge set of product-on-body images that helps a visitor understand scale or use.
6. One or two object stories pairing concise text with a still-life or material detail.
7. A large image chapter that resets visual scale and showcases a real approved product or collection.
8. A short operational ledger for verified shipping, returns, care, appointment, or customer-service details, only where supplied.
9. A footer browse taxonomy organized around real product categories, materials, use, or collections, plus required legal and contact links.

Represent retail categories through useful names and destination links, not an arbitrary mass of filters. A new local jeweler may need appointments, repair, resizing, or custom work; an ecommerce shop may need collections, search, variants, and cart. Choose the model that facts support. Product prices, materials, care, origin, stone sourcing, stock status, availability, and delivery policies must come from verified content tokens. SEO language should target the actual category and location intent without making unsupported quality or provenance promises.

## Responsive translation

At 1440px, keep the initial portrait composition full-bleed or nearly edge-to-edge and hold the navigation in a narrow strip. Make the statement band a distinct spatial pause. At 390px, stack portraits as full-width editorial frames instead of squeezing both into tiny columns. Keep the statement between image chapters, then stack text and object image in a clear reading order. Convert taxonomy columns into clearly labeled expandable groups, with search and cart still visible or one tap away. Avoid fixed-width product rails that produce page-level horizontal overflow.

Use image changes and whitespace as the primary movement. Optional image crossfades or a slow announcement ticker should not delay shopping or hide information. Keep carousel controls visible and keyboard-operable. Under reduced motion, freeze the ticker and expose all content statically. Maintain readable text contrast, descriptive alt text, visible focus, semantic navigation labels, and a predictable tab order.

## Signature elements

- Portrait-led opening that treats product photography as the main visual hierarchy.
- A clean, centered editorial statement that separates two image chapters.
- Concise object narratives paired with carefully selected still-life imagery.
- A material and category taxonomy that makes shopping discoverable.
- A compact factual service ledger when the business has verified retail policies or appointment details.

## Prohibited patterns

- No copied source logo, product names, collection names, product forms, source copy, photography, exact layout details, or trademarked identity.
- No invented metal grades, stone origin, ethical sourcing, sustainability, handcraft claims, product scarcity, appointment availability, price, shipping threshold, return window, warranty, or testimonials.
- No stock people portrayed as real makers, customers, or employees.
- No generic round-card catalog, no oversized text-only split hero, no hover-only navigation, and no parallax or ticker that harms readability.
- No product-photo crop that hides an important differentiating feature, and no source image reused as client imagery.

Before delivery, inspect the opening at 100 percent zoom and the full page at desktop and mobile sizes. Verify category navigation, search/cart placement, policy facts, keyboard interaction, reduced motion, image descriptions, and zero horizontal overflow. Compare only the composition grammar with the reference; client facts, SEO content, and approved images remain authoritative.
