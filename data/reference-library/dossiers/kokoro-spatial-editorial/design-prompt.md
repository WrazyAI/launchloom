# Kokoro spatial architecture editorial: archive-only design implementation brief

This document extracts visual mechanics from the permission-cleared source captures. It is not source copy and does not grant permission to reuse any source brand asset. The dossier is archive-only and is not eligible for automatic production selection.

## visual hierarchy
Create a quiet, spatial architecture story using a monumental, multi-level editorial identity, ample but purposeful negative space, and large interior imagery. Establish two clearly distinct typographic roles in the opening identity, then let one portrait interior sit alone before a centered thesis. Any secondary accent must use an independent type treatment; do not imitate the source's handwritten wordmark or signature color. Alternate wide room scenes and a magazine-like archive ledger, then close on one immersive interior and a clear inquiry. It should feel like a considered architecture journal, not a split hero plus a generic service grid.

## page sequence
Use this sequence: quiet opposing-corner navigation; monumental two-level identity; one or more portrait space images; a separate concise architectural thesis and first inquiry action; wide selected-work scene rail; project or insight ledger with metadata only when verified; one cinematic closing image; direct studio inquiry and minimal footer. Do not render the client's hero kicker or thesis directly beneath the identity before the first portrait. Move that verified copy to the distinct thesis section after the image. Adapt archive rows to real work, articles, or process notes available in the client facts. Do not invent a magazine to fill space.

## responsive translation
On desktop, keep the identity broad, approaching 0.93 of the 1440px viewport width, and give it deliberate vertical presence before the first image. Do not force a long business name into one undersized line. If the verified name needs room, use a deliberate two-line break or a two-level lockup that preserves the dominant scale; never shrink the whole identity just to avoid wrapping. Use only the submitted brand name and verified descriptor. Keep the secondary role clearly subordinate and typographically distinct, but do not reproduce the source's handwritten treatment, exact letterforms, logo, or accent styling. Place a small, factual service-area or business-context label in the upper-left corner and Services, FAQs, Contact as quiet native links in the opposing upper-right corner. Do not repeat the full studio name in a tiny header above the same monumental identity.

On mobile, keep a factual service-area label at the upper left and a compact Menu control at the upper right; expose Services, FAQs, Contact, and the inquiry path inside a touch-friendly menu. If no verified location is available, omit the label instead of inventing one. Do not place a duplicate tiny wordmark in the header above the large identity. Keep navigation much smaller than both identity levels.

The first interior image is a tall architectural scene with breakpoint-specific geometry. At 1440px desktop, its frame is about 0.74 width-to-height, 0.44 viewport width, and 0.95 viewport height, or about 0.42 of the viewport area. It begins around 0.79 viewport heights below the top, so only its upper portion appears in the first 900px viewport and the full frame continues below the fold. Preserve that wordmark-to-image rhythm; do not shrink the image to force its full frame above the fold. At 390px mobile, the image frame is about 0.46 width-to-height, 0.92 viewport width, and 0.93 viewport height. It begins around 0.29 viewport heights below the top and continues below the first viewport. In both layouts, the photograph must fill its own frame edge to edge; preserve the pale page space outside the frame, with no blank image panel or empty extension.

At mobile, keep the identity broad within 390px, around 0.90 viewport width, and visually substantial above the large image frame. Let a long name wrap at a meaningful word boundary instead of reducing it to a small single line; retain the two-level hierarchy without copying the source accent style. Stack image scenes and make project rows large enough for touch. Keep the first viewport focused on navigation, identity, and the entering portrait scene. Place the separate thesis and primary inquiry action after the portrait, never underneath the wordmark or over the image. Keep a clear inquiry path in the menu and repeat it after the thesis. Avoid a large pill button over photography. Preserve readable long-form text and the ending image without horizontal page scroll.

## signature elements
Required mechanics: monumental two-level typographic identity; restrained navigation split across opposing corners on desktop and recomposed as service-area label plus Menu on mobile; isolated architecture imagery; a thesis after the portrait scene; an archive/ledger transition; and a distinctive closing interior scene. Use the client's original identity and images or newly generated architectural imagery; never use the Kokoro logo, script, photographs, article list, or copy.

## prohibited patterns
Do not replicate Kokoro branding, wordmark, gold script, photographs, magazine titles, dates, contact details, layout pixel-for-pixel, or source claims. No fabricated portfolio projects, awards, locations, sustainability performance, or dimensions. Do not make contact dependent on finding a tiny email in the footer; keep a useful inquiry action visible and factual.

## local-business translation and SEO
For a LaunchLoom client, translate this composition only after grounding the page in the client's verified business kind, services, service area, customer questions, contact channels, assets, and SEO research. Keep the opening statement useful to the actual searcher, use natural service/location language, answer the real decision questions, and place one primary conversion action early. Never copy source copy, logos, people, photos, products, claims, or interface assets. Any missing fact remains absent or is requested from the client; do not fill it with a plausible invention. Keep semantic headings, keyboard and touch access, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion behavior, and a working form or call action.

### Type, surfaces and image roles

- Display character: high-contrast editorial serif identity in two deliberate typographic levels with an independent secondary type role; no copied script treatment. Body character: quiet neutral sans. Hierarchy: broad two-level identity with deliberate line breaks for long verified names; visually substantial before the first image, with quiet navigation, medium centered thesis, very small archive metadata, and restrained body copy. Translate these roles to the host's local font catalog and honor client-selected heading/body families. Specify a responsive scale, line-height and intentional wrapping; keep body lines under about 80 characters. Do not copy source font files or fetch remote fonts.
- Source surface cues: soft warm gray; ivory; deep charcoal; natural wood. Contrast intent: photographs supply warmth; serif text remains dark and legible against clear untextured surfaces. Propose 4-6 named client-compatible color tokens with hex values in the contract, labeled as implementation choices, not measured source colors. Bind them to the host's accessible per-surface palette roles rather than overriding client brand variables.
- Media role: architectural interiors treated as calm spatial objects. Crop guidance: portrait hero/interior images, wide overlapping scene strip, then one full-bleed closing room. Focal role: structure, natural light, material and proportion. Map opening/secondary/detail placements to the available client image tokens. Preserve role, focal point and mobile crop without using reference screenshots as site assets. If the right media is absent, use an allowed contextual asset or deliberate graphic treatment, and flag any signature that cannot be met; never imply stock people or spaces belong to the client.
- Service presentation: architecture capabilities are translated into an editorial project/archive index, not generic service cards. Early action: place the first inquiry action after the portrait image and a clearly separate centered thesis; keep only compact native route links in desktop navigation and do not put the thesis under the identity or a large CTA over photography. Primary placement: Discuss a project or verified studio inquiry after the separate thesis and again at the closing scene. Name the actual client next step; repeat it at decision points, not mechanically after every chapter.

### Mobile and interaction translation

Recorded mobile strategy: **vertical image-led architecture essay with ledger rows**. scale the wordmark without horizontal clipping; keep image proportions tall and intentional; stack the scene strip or use a controlled touch rail; make the archive ledger readable and tappable; place inquiry after the thesis and final scene; preserve enough negative space without empty artificial sections.

Record separately what stacks, reorders, changes crop, wraps, becomes a menu or becomes a touch control. Do not just shrink desktop. Keep the primary action, visible focus, labels and form controls usable without hover or motion; no horizontal overflow or competing sticky overlays. Recorded interaction opportunity: slow image reveal or restrained crop transition with a subtle horizontal scene pan. Reduced-motion contract: settled full images, no pinned scroll or continuous camera movement. Implement only the justified documented interaction, with equivalent static content. Do not add repeated fade-up entrances, autoplay or section pinning to compensate for an unclear layout.
