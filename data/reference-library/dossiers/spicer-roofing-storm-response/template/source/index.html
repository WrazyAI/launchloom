Five steps, and you only do three of them.

Most roof replacements are an insurance conversation before they are a construction one. Here is exactly where the homeowner has to act and where we take it off your hands.

  1. 01

    Inspection & documentation

    You do

    Point us at the storm date and unlock the side gate.

    We do

    We walk the roof, chalk and photograph every hit, measure the slopes and put it into the format an adjuster reads.

  2. 02

    Claim filed

    You do

    Make the call to your carrier — the policyholder has to open it, not the contractor.

    We do

    We send you the damage report and the exact loss date and cause wording to read out on that call.

  3. 03

    Adjuster meeting

    You do

    Nothing. You do not need to be on the ladder or in the attic.

    We do

    We meet the adjuster on site, walk the roof with them and hand over the photograph set so nothing gets missed.

  4. 04

    Scope agreed

    You do

    Approve the final scope and pay your deductible, which is yours by law.

    We do

    We reconcile the carrier's scope against what the roof actually needs and supplement anything left off it.

  5. 05

    Build

    You do

    Move the cars off the drive the night before.

    We do

    Tear off, dry in, replace rotted decking, install the system, magnet-sweep the property twice and close the claim out.

Asked on every call

The five questions, answered straight.

01

Will my insurance cover this?

If the damage is storm-related, usually yes. The inspection produces the documentation an adjuster needs — dated photographs, a slope-by-slope hit count and a measured diagram — and we meet the adjuster on site so the scope is argued once rather than three times.

02

How long does a replacement take?

Most residential tear-offs and replacements are one to two days on site, weather permitting. Larger or steeper roofs, or anything with significant decking rot underneath, run to three. We give you the honest number before the crew is booked rather than after.

03

What warranty comes with it?

A workmanship warranty from us plus the manufacturer's system warranty, which requires certified installation. Both are in writing and both are handed over on the day the job closes, not posted out at some later date.

04

Do I still have to pay my deductible?

Yes, and be wary of anyone who tells you otherwise. Absorbing or rebating a deductible is insurance fraud in most states and it voids the claim it was meant to help. Your deductible is the only part of a covered replacement you pay.

05

What happens if it rains halfway through the tear-off?

We only strip what the crew can dry in the same day, so there is always a watertight underlayment over open decking before anyone leaves. If the forecast turns hard we stop stripping and secure what is open rather than racing the weather.
