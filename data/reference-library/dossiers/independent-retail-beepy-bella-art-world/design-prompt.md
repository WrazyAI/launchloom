# Artist-led independent-retail visual implementation brief

Use the paired direct-site screenshots as evidence of visual mechanics only. Create a new, original retail world based on the client's verified products, artist story, categories, and SEO research. Do not copy Beepy Bella's brand, product names, drawings, wordmark, catalog images, exact language, product art, or trade dress. Client assets take priority; otherwise generate or source new images under approved rights and ensure every image represents the client's real category.

## Visual hierarchy

Build an eccentric but controlled product stage rather than a standard storefront hero. Pair one dramatic close product image with one smaller isolated object framed by circular type, line marks, or another product-relevant graphic. The two visual weights should feel intentionally unequal. The first impression should identify what kind of objects the business sells without requiring a large marketing paragraph. Surrounding navigation can be unusually explicit and modular: use thin ruled divisions, a simple wordmark, shop/about destinations, and compact currency/search/cart utilities as supported by the client's commerce model.

Use white as a gallery field and black as a sharp structural counterpoint. Let a small number of saturated colors come from the client's own product art. Use clean sans-serif typography for commerce and metadata, then allow restrained playful labels or curved type to add personality. Circular text must remain readable and must not be the only way a product or action is named. Do not imitate the source's particular characters, illustrations, products, or exact colors as a branded palette.

## Page sequence

1. Segmented store navigation with concise major destinations and touch-friendly utilities.
2. Asymmetric product stage: one high-impact crop and one isolated product visual in a graphic orbit or similarly original frame.
3. Curated frontpage product selection with clear names, verified price/availability tokens, and direct detail links.
4. Optional video or collection interlude. If no approved video exists, use a generated/client-owned poster or a static product still; never reproduce a blank or blocked media area.
5. Artist/maker story paired with a large image or user-controlled image slider. Use only verified founder, process, location, and material facts.
6. A structured category directory spanning the client's actual product families. Use useful group labels rather than an enormous undifferentiated list.
7. Mailing-list or contact close with a clear consent explanation and privacy link.

Create a real shopping hierarchy: category, collection, product detail, and cart route should be obvious. If the business does not have ecommerce, replace price and cart details with the correct inquiry or appointment action. SEO titles, product descriptions, and category copy should use terms grounded in the client's real offer and service area. Never import the reference store's prices, product claims, founder biography, production methods, or wellness claims.

## Responsive translation

At 1440px, align the header as a thin ruled bar and let the product stage use contrasting image scales. Contain all content within the layout viewport; the reference's body width extended to 3585px at a 1440px viewport and that defect must not be repeated. At 390px, condense the header to brand, search/cart, and menu. Center the orbital object before or after a full-width product crop; do not create a tiny desktop-like split. Use a two-column product grid only if product titles and prices remain readable, then stack the maker story and media. Collapse long directories into labeled groups and test every menu state for overflow.

Use object rotation, orbital text, and image slider motion sparingly. Every carousel needs visible controls and a static selected state. Under reduced motion, stop the orbit, restore native scrolling, and reveal all product/story content without requiring scroll-triggered animation. Never autoplay sound or lock the user into a scroll sequence. Ensure accessible labels for search, basket, and currency, plus keyboard-accessible menus, visible focus, useful alt text, and sufficient contrast.

## Signature elements

- An original product-centered opening with asymmetrical image scale and a circular or orbit-like framing device.
- An unusual but useful segmented navigation that exposes true store categories and commerce utilities.
- A product grid that arrives after the opening stage, with clean metadata and clear direct actions.
- A verified maker/process story paired with a large art-directed image or controlled slider.
- A category directory that feels like a curated world and remains easy to use on mobile.

## Prohibited patterns

- No copied brand, wordmark, drawings, source photos, characters, exact product names, product art, text, or trade dress.
- No desktop body wider than the viewport, no clipped navigation, and no black media void presented as intentional art direction.
- No invented products, price, founder story, manufacturing location, materials, health or healing effect, scarcity, reviews, or availability.
- No source images repackaged as client assets, no generic card wall, no inaccessible text-on-a-circle, and no autoplay audio or scroll lock.
- No unsupported product filters, currency options, loyalty tools, or cart behavior.

Before delivery, inspect desktop and mobile at 100 percent zoom. Verify product hierarchy, category navigation, action links, checkout/inquiry truth, image rights, page-width containment, focus states, reduced motion, and source-fact isolation. Preserve the visual contrast and art-world energy, not the original site's faults or identity.
