# Visual hierarchy

Use the supplied direct-site screenshots and Reference DNA as visual evidence for a fitness experience, not as a page to copy. Do not reproduce Future's logo, app screens, exact photography, colors, text, coach/member identities, testimonials, or product trade dress. The source is a digital personal-training product rather than a physical local gym. For a new client's verified local business, translate its product-story sequence into an unmistakably local fitness service. Lead with an oversized editorial proposition over an atmospheric lifestyle image and a visible phone or session artifact that previews how the service works. Place a short announcement or offer above the hero only when real client facts support it. Use a simple wordmark/nav, one primary CTA, a large but readable headline, and brief supporting copy. Keep the mobile view from becoming a copy-heavy stack or a screen full of overlapping product mockups.

# Page sequence

Preserve the observed narrative mechanics: hero promise and introductory CTA; a second high-impact statement for the value proposition; a horizontally browsable feature/benefit carousel; a dedicated trainer or coaching introduction; a structured personal roadmap showing what inputs shape a plan; a results/social-proof area only when verified; a coach or class-discovery action; and a clear close with contact and local details. Translate each source section into confirmed client information such as training format, coaching process, class options, membership type, accessibility, or local service area. Do not carry Future's digital subscription offer, personal trainer availability, member outcomes, testimonials, workout names, equipment, health claims, or app functionality into a gym website unless the client independently confirms an equivalent. Keep a service, FAQs, and contact path available even if the source has a quiz or coach matching tool.

# Responsive translation

On desktop, preserve the opening's layered balance between headline, human photo, and device/product preview; keep the first action above the fold. Use wide benefit cards and a clear transition into the coaching roadmap. At 390 px, stack the phone/product stage beneath or beside the hero copy without hiding the CTA; keep benefit cards horizontally swipeable with clear focus and touch behavior; make roadmap details readable as vertical bands; and ensure all media has a non-autoplay still fallback. Do not force a desktop carousel to overflow the entire page. Use native horizontal scrolling or accessible buttons with focus states. Ensure the page stays at or below viewport width, forms and booking links are touch-sized, and footer groups stack cleanly. Respect reduced motion by removing autoplay, scroll-driven transitions, and parallax while keeping every section and link visible.

# Signature elements

Preserve the product-story cadence: announcement strip, personal promise, phone or product preview, benefit carousel, coach introduction, structured roadmap, results section, and a repeated coach-finding action. One purposeful interaction should help a visitor understand the service, such as a user-controlled feature carousel or a short intake/goal chooser. Avoid autoplay motion that hides information. For a local gym, replace "coach matching" with the actual next step such as a trial, consult, class selection, or visit, using client-confirmed facts. Use client photography where available; otherwise generate imagery that depicts the real local service context and does not imply invented coaches, customers, results, or facilities. A local business needs actual NAP, location/service area, and FAQs from verified content rather than the remote service's contact model.

# Prohibited patterns

Do not clone Future's brand, app screens, people, member testimonials, exact wording, pricing, claims, phone mockup, or visual trade dress. Do not call a digital training platform a local gym. Do not invent coach assignment, trainer credentials, fitness outcomes, member counts, trial terms, class schedules, equipment, subscription costs, or neighborhood coverage. Avoid generic bento grids, inaccessible auto-advancing carousels, huge blank space that pushes the CTA down, dense text over faces, and app screens that the client does not have. Never use an em dash in rendered copy.

# Content and SEO constraints

Use client-confirmed training type and service location naturally in title, H1, opening copy, and service details. Keep one H1 and useful H2s for services, coaching/process, FAQs, and contact. Write practical, concise answers to real local search questions: what sessions include, who they suit, how to start, where training happens, and what the visitor needs to bring. Bind price, trial, location, coach, schedule, and claims to sealed intake/research tokens. Use appropriate local business schema and canonicals through the production shell. The source is an online coaching product; its details are not SEO facts for another provider.
